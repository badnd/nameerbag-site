Most delayed orders were not produced slowly. They were started late.

Buyers tend to plan forward from the day they decide, and then discover the calendar does not reach. The reliable method is the opposite: fix the date the bags have to be in your hands, and count backwards through every block that has to fit before it — including the two blocks people forget, which are your own review time and the shipping transit.

This guide gives you the blocks and the arithmetic.

## The Blocks You Have to Fit In

| Block | Duration | Who controls it |
|---|---|---|
| Specification and quotation | Days, depending on how complete your brief is | You and us |
| Sampling | **7–15 days** | Us |
| Your sample review | However long you take | 🔴 **You** |
| Corrections and re-sample, if needed | Another sampling cycle | Both |
| Bulk production | **15–30 days** | Us |
| Shipping transit | Varies by method and destination | Your freight arrangement |
| Customs and inland delivery | Varies by country | Outside anyone's control |

**Two of these blocks are yours, and they are the ones that slip.** The production clock does not run while a sample sits unreviewed or artwork is unconfirmed. In our experience the difference between an order that lands on time and one that does not is almost never the factory.

**Shipping is not a number we can promise.** Transit time depends on your freight forwarder, the shipping method, the route and the destination. Get a transit estimate from your freight contact early and build it into your plan — it is often the largest block on the whole calendar.

## What Actually Happens During Bulk

You do not need the full workshop detail to plan, but you do need to know where the change deadlines sit.

**Order confirmed → materials purchased → fabric cut → branding applied to the cut panels → panels prepared for the line → sewing → end-of-line inspection → packing → shipment.**

Two deadlines matter to you:

- **Once fabric is cut, dimension changes are no longer practical.** A size change after cutting means new material and a new schedule.
- **Artwork must be final before branding is applied.** Branding goes onto the cut panels before assembly, so a late logo change means reprinting panels rather than adjusting a finished bag.

Everything else is ours to manage. Ask for progress photos if you want visibility.

## Working Backwards — A Worked Example

Suppose you need bags in hand for an event.

| Counting backwards from your in-hand date | |
|---|---|
| Customs and inland delivery | Allow a buffer — varies by country |
| Shipping transit | Get the figure from your freight contact |
| Bulk production | 15–30 days |
| Your final approval and order placement | Allow a few days |
| Sample review and any corrections | Allow at least one full extra sampling cycle |
| Sampling | 7–15 days |
| Specification and quotation | Allow a week for a first project |

**Add a buffer, and put it at the end rather than the middle.** A plan with no slack fails on the first small surprise. A plan with a week of slack absorbs one and still lands.

**If the arithmetic does not reach, tell us before you order.** Sometimes we can simplify a specification to save a sampling round. Sometimes the honest answer is that the date is not achievable, and we would rather say so than take the order and disappoint you.

## When to Start, by Scenario

Different programmes have different pressure points. These are the ones we see most.

| Scenario | Plan around |
|---|---|
| Back-to-school retail | Work backwards from your shelf date, not the school start date — retail needs stock in advance |
| Seasonal camps and programmes | These order in a predictable window each year; starting before your competitors do protects your capacity |
| Trade show or event giveaway | The date cannot move, so build the largest buffer here of any scenario |
| Retail launch | Allow a full extra sampling cycle — retail specifications change more during review |
| Corporate gifting for year-end | Q4 is the busiest period across the industry; start earlier than feels necessary |
| Repeat order of an approved style | The fastest case — no sampling if nothing has changed |

**Two industry-wide constraints worth knowing.** Capacity across the industry tightens before major seasonal deadlines, and Chinese factories close for an extended period around Chinese New Year, with dates that move each year. Both are predictable, and both catch out buyers who plan only from their own calendar. Confirm current dates with Anna when you plan.

## How to Make Your Order Go Faster

- **Send a complete brief.** Dimensions, fabric, colours, branding method and quantity in one message removes a round of questions.
- **Send final vector artwork with your order**, not after it.
- **Give sample feedback in one consolidated round** rather than across several messages.
- **Give us Pantone coated codes early**, not after sampling — colour matching can require dye trials.
- **Tell us your in-hand date at the start**, not your ship date. We plan backwards from it.
- **Repeat an approved style where you can.** A repeat order with no changes skips sampling entirely.

## FAQ

**How long does a custom bag order take from start to delivery?**
Sampling takes 7–15 days and bulk production takes 15–30 days. On top of that you need to allow for specification and quotation, your own sample review time, and shipping transit — which depends on your freight arrangement and destination. Plan backwards from your in-hand date rather than forwards from today.

**When should I place my order for a specific deadline?**
Count backwards through every block: customs and inland delivery, shipping transit, bulk production of 15–30 days, order placement, sample review and possible corrections, sampling of 7–15 days, and specification. Then add a buffer at the end. If the arithmetic does not reach your date, tell us before you order and we will say honestly whether it is achievable.

**What is the most common cause of a late order?**
Waiting for the customer. The production clock stops while a sample sits unreviewed or artwork is unconfirmed. The second most common cause is sample feedback arriving in several separate rounds, each of which triggers another sampling cycle.

**How long does shipping take?**
Transit time depends on your shipping method, route, destination and freight forwarder, so it is not a figure we can promise. Get an estimate from your freight contact early — it is often the largest single block in your timeline.

**Can I still change the design once bulk production has started?**
Small changes may be possible early on. Once the fabric has been cut, a dimension change means new material and a new schedule. Artwork must be final before branding is applied to the cut panels. We will always tell you when a request has passed that point rather than absorbing it silently.

**Do repeat orders take less time?**
Yes, significantly. A repeat of an approved style with no changes skips sampling entirely and goes straight to bulk production of 15–30 days. This is the single biggest timeline saving available to you.

**Does Chinese New Year affect my order?**
Factories across China close for an extended period around Chinese New Year, and the dates move each year. Capacity also tightens across the industry ahead of major seasonal deadlines. Both are predictable if you plan for them — confirm the current dates with Anna when you are planning your calendar.
