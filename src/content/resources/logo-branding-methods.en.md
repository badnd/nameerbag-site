"Can you put our logo on it" is the easiest question to ask and the hardest to answer well. The right method depends on your artwork, the fabric, where on the bag it sits, how many you are ordering and how long it needs to survive. Get the match right and the branding looks intentional. Get it wrong and you end up with a cracked print on a curved panel, or embroidery that turned your fine typography into a fuzzy blob.

This guide covers every branding method we run in-house, what each is genuinely good at, and the traps that come up most often.

## All Methods at a Glance

| Method | Best for | Colour handling | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screen printing | Bold logos, large flat areas, simple shapes | Solid spot colours; cost rises with colour count | Sits on the surface |
| Heat transfer | Detailed or photographic artwork, small runs | Full colour, gradients possible | Smooth film on the surface |
| Embroidery | Premium look, durability, text and simple marks | Thread colours matched to your artwork | Raised and textured |
| Woven label | Brand marks, care and origin info, inside and outside | Multiple thread colours, fine detail | Fabric, stitched on |
| Private label | Full own-brand presentation | Your label spec throughout | Retail-ready |
| Rubber / PVC patch | Outdoor and streetwear look, 3D effect | Moulded colours, layered relief | Raised, rubbery |
| Leather patch | Heritage and premium positioning | Debossed or printed | Firm, tactile |
| Metal badge | Hardware-level branding, permanence | Plating finishes | Solid metal |
| Custom zipper pull | Subtle branding buyers notice when using the bag | Moulded or plated | Functional detail |
| Pantone dyeing | Matching the bag body to your brand colour | Whole fabric dyed to target | The fabric itself |
| Reflective print | Visibility on outdoor and sports bags | Silver-grey by day, bright under light | Printed layer |

**All-over print (dye sublimation)** is also available for full-coverage designs on polyester. It is a fabric-level decoration rather than a logo application, so it is quoted differently — ask Anna if your design covers whole panels rather than a placed mark.

## Matching Method to Situation

**You have a bold two-colour logo on a flat panel.** Screen printing. It is the workhorse for a reason — clean edges, strong colour, holds up well.

**Your artwork is photographic or has gradients.** Screen printing cannot do this cleanly. Use heat transfer for a placed graphic, or all-over print if the design covers the panel.

**You want it to look expensive.** Embroidery, leather patch or metal badge. These read as considered rather than printed, and buyers pick up on it immediately.

**Your logo has very fine text.** Not embroidery. Stitching cannot resolve small letterforms and they close up into a solid mass. Use a woven label, heat transfer or screen printing instead.

**It is going on a curved or padded surface.** Avoid large solid prints — they crack where the fabric flexes. A patch, badge or embroidered mark handles curvature far better.

**It has to survive heavy outdoor use.** Embroidery, rubber patch or metal badge. Prints wear on high-abrasion contact points over time.

**You want the bag body itself in your brand colour.** Pantone dyeing. This changes the fabric rather than adding a mark on top, and it is the only way to get a true brand-colour body.

**It is a night-time or sports product.** Reflective print, usually alongside a second method for daytime visibility.

**You want it to look like your own product, not a blank with a logo.** Private label — your own labels, hangtags and packaging throughout. This is the difference between a promotional bag and a retail product.

## What Each Method Needs From You

| Method | Artwork needed | Practical minimum size |
|---|---|---|
| Screen printing | Vector, colours separated, Pantone coated codes | 15 mm for simple marks |
| Heat transfer | Vector or 300 dpi raster at final size | 10 mm |
| Embroidery | Vector; we convert to a stitch file for your approval | 20 mm; text 5 mm letter height |
| Woven label | Vector, with label dimensions specified | Label 20 × 40 mm typical |
| Rubber / PVC patch | Vector, layers marked for relief | 25 mm |
| Leather patch | Vector, debossed or printed specified | 25 mm |
| Metal badge | Vector, plating finish specified | 15 mm |
| Custom zipper pull | Vector, with the zipper size it fits | Set by the zipper size |
| Pantone dyeing | Pantone coated code | Applies to the whole fabric |
| Reflective print | Vector, simple shapes work best | 15 mm |

**Send vector wherever possible** — AI, EPS, PDF or SVG, with text converted to outlines. A vector logo can be used for every method on this page. A small JPG pulled from a website usually cannot be used for any of them without being redrawn.

## What Drives Cost

No prices appear on this page — every project is quoted on its own specification. But it helps to know which decisions move the number, so you can adjust before you ask.

| Decision | Effect |
|---|---|
| Number of colours in a screen print | Each additional colour adds a screen and a pass |
| Stitch count in embroidery | A dense, detailed design takes far longer than a simple mark |
| Number of branding positions | Each placement is a separate operation |
| Moulded items (rubber patch, metal badge, zipper pull) | Require tooling, which favours larger quantities |
| Pantone dyeing | Applies at fabric level, so it suits larger runs better than small ones |
| Quantity | The single biggest factor across every method |

**A practical note.** If you are testing a design at a small quantity, methods that avoid tooling — heat transfer, embroidery, woven label, screen printing — let you start smaller and change your mind cheaply. Moulded branding is worth committing to once the design is settled.

## The Mistakes We See Most

- **Sending a website logo file.** A 200-pixel PNG from a homepage cannot be used at bag scale for any method. Ask your designer for the original vector.
- **Embroidering fine text.** It always looks worse than the mock-up suggests. Test it at actual size before committing.
- **Approving a screen colour.** Screens and fabric never match exactly. Approve colour on the physical sample, not the layout.
- **Large solid print on a flex point.** Prints crack where fabric bends repeatedly. Move it, shrink it, or switch method.
- **Branding placed too near a seam.** Anything within about 20 mm of a trim line risks being caught in a seam or binding. Tell us the bag style so we can position it correctly.
- **Choosing method before fabric.** Some combinations do not work well together. Decide the bag and fabric first, then the branding.

## FAQ

**Which branding method is most durable?**
Embroidery, rubber patches and metal badges last longest because the branding is stitched or fixed rather than applied to the surface. Prints hold up well in normal use but wear faster at high-abrasion contact points on heavily used outdoor bags.

**What file format should I send for my logo?**
Vector artwork — AI, EPS, PDF or SVG — with all text converted to outlines. A vector file works for every method on this page. A small raster image taken from a website usually has to be redrawn before it can be used.

**Can you match my exact brand colour?**
Send us your Pantone coated code and we will match as closely as the method allows. Different methods reproduce colour differently — thread, ink and dyed fabric each behave differently against the same code — so confirm colour on the physical sample rather than on a screen.

**Can I use more than one branding method on the same bag?**
Yes, and it is common. An embroidered mark on the front with a woven label inside and a custom zipper pull is a typical retail combination. Each placement is a separate operation, so tell us all of them when you request a quotation.

**My logo has very small text. What method should I use?**
Not embroidery — stitching cannot resolve small letterforms and they close up. A woven label, heat transfer or screen printing will hold fine detail much better at small sizes.

**Do I need a bigger order for some branding methods?**
Moulded branding — rubber patches, metal badges and custom zipper pulls — requires tooling, which suits larger quantities better. Printing, embroidery and woven labels are more practical at smaller quantities. We work from 50 pcs\* as a starting point and Anna will confirm what is practical for your chosen method in your quotation.

**What is private label and how is it different from putting a logo on a bag?**
A logo application places your mark on an otherwise standard bag. Private label means the product is presented as yours throughout — your own labels, hangtags and packaging, with no reference to us anywhere on the product. It is what turns a branded bag into a retail product.

**Can you make the whole bag in my brand colour?**
Yes, through Pantone dyeing, which dyes the fabric itself rather than printing on top. Because it applies at fabric level it suits larger runs better than small test orders. Send your Pantone coated code and Anna will confirm what is practical for your quantity.
